# Compliance Requirements Across Markets

Every country has its own rules for what makes an imported vehicle road-legal. The tax number is only half the story — conformity is the other half, and it varies materially by market. This page consolidates the requirements so you can plan before you order.

> **How to read this page.** Each market section states (a) the approval regime and the authority that runs it, (b) the typical approval route for a China-market EV, (c) the common adaptation points, and (d) how to verify the current state before committing. Requirements change; every section below is a planning aid, not a guarantee. Confirm the current text and the exact route with the relevant authority or an accredited technical service before ordering.

## The four regimes at a glance

| Market | Approval regime | Authority / basis | Typical vehicle types | Cost (USD, illustrative) | Timeline (typical) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EU | WVTA / Small Series / national single-vehicle | EU type-approval framework; national approval authorities | all | $1,500–$5,000 | 2–6 weeks |
| UK | IVA (Individual Vehicle Approval) | DVSA | all | $1,500–$4,000 | 2–5 weeks |
| UAE / Saudi | GCC conformity + national standards | national authorities (GSO-aligned standards) | all | market-specific | 1–4 weeks |
| Australia | ADR + RAV entry | Department of Infrastructure (RAV) | all | higher; specialist | 2–8 weeks |

## Europe (EU)

The EU's framework is the most mature: a vehicle must satisfy the applicable technical requirements under the EU type-approval system and, in practice, pass one of the approval routes — full Whole Vehicle Type Approval (WVTA), Small Series, or a national single-vehicle approval. See [EU Homologation](/docs/eu-homologation/) for the routes in detail.

**What this means for a China-market EV:**

- A vehicle approved only for the Chinese domestic market does **not** automatically carry any EU approval. An existing export-market type approval (some Chinese brands homologate models for the EU themselves) is the fastest path — verify the specific VIN range and approval number, not just the brand.
- If no EU approval exists, the route is a national single-vehicle approval (e.g. Germany's §21 StVZO procedure via TÜV/DEKRA/GTÜ) or Small Series where volume qualifies.
- The adaptation list is predictable but non-trivial: lighting conformity, speedometer/display format, charging-port conformity, and documentation of component approvals.

**Verification steps:**

1. Ask the supplier for the EU Certificate of Conformity (CoC) or existing type-approval certificate — by VIN.
2. If none exists, obtain a written conformity assessment from an accredited technical service before paying for the vehicle.
3. Confirm with the destination member state's approval authority which route applies at your volume.

## United Kingdom

Post-Brexit, the UK runs its own approval regime, the **Individual Vehicle Approval (IVA)** scheme administered by the DVSA. A China-market EV generally needs a Basic IVA; vehicles with an existing EU type approval may qualify for a simplified route, but this depends on the specific approval status and cannot be assumed.

Common adaptation points mirror the EU — lighting, a mph-capable speedometer display, and right-hand-drive considerations where relevant — plus UK-specific documentation requirements. See [UK Import](/docs/uk-import/).

**Verification steps:**

1. Confirm whether the vehicle carries a recognised EU/UK type approval that DVSA will accept (mutual recognition is route-specific, not automatic).
2. Book an IVA inspection through the DVSA channel and confirm the category (Basic IVA vs other) for your vehicle type.
3. Confirm the adaptation list with the IVA inspection centre before shipping — some items require parts that are slow to source.

## Gulf (UAE & Saudi Arabia)

The Gulf applies its own conformity framework, aligned with GCC standards and enforced by national authorities. Vehicles typically need to demonstrate compliance with the relevant GCC/GSO technical standards and the destination country's national requirements. The process is generally lighter than the EU's, but the requirements are **not identical between the UAE and Saudi Arabia** — and they can differ by emirate and by vehicle category.

**What this means for a China-market EV:**

- A China-market vehicle may pass Gulf conformity with fewer modifications than the EU requires, but "fewer" is not "none": lighting, labelling, and emissions-adjacent documentation still come up.
- The Gulf's climate adds a de-facto requirement no certificate covers: battery thermal management and AC performance in 45°C+ conditions. That is a product-fitness question, not a paperwork question — and it is the reason some models are simply not imported into the region.

**Verification steps:**

1. Confirm the current conformity route and required standards with the destination authority (or a local clearance agent) — before ordering.
2. Ask the supplier which GCC markets the specific model has already been exported to, and request the conformity documents from those exports.
3. Do not assume UAE clearance equals Saudi clearance; the two processes are separate.

## Australia

Australia is the most demanding on documentation. Imported vehicles must meet the **Australian Design Rules (ADRs)** and be entered on the **Register of Approved Vehicles (RAV)**, usually via a concessional entry for vehicles not type-approved for Australia. Physical modifications (lighting, child-restraint anchors, speedometer calibration) are frequently required, and the evidentiary bar for conformity is the highest of the four markets covered here.

**What this means for a China-market EV:**

- A China-market EV generally does not automatically satisfy ADRs, and there is no "light" version of the process — budget more time and cost than for the Gulf.
- Some models have been approved for Australia by their manufacturers (RAV-listed); if the specific variant is RAV-listed, the path is far shorter. Check the RAV, not the marketing material.

**Verification steps:**

1. Search the RAV for the exact variant (make, model, and compliance date) before planning around it.
2. If the variant is not RAV-listed, engage a registered automotive workshop / RAV assessor early to scope the physical modification list.
3. Confirm the concessional entry category with the Department of Infrastructure; eligibility conditions are specific and change.

## A practical checklist

Before committing to any import, confirm these five things:

1. **Does the specific vehicle have an existing approval in the target market?** (Some Chinese brands already type-approve models for export markets — this is the fastest path. Verify by VIN or variant, not by brand name.)
2. **What is the required approval route for your volume?** Single vehicle, small series, or full type approval.
3. **What physical adaptations will the vehicle need?** Lighting, display, safety anchor points — get the list in writing before shipment.
4. **Who will perform the conformity assessment?** An accredited technical service (TÜV, DEKRA, GTÜ, DVSA, RAV assessor, or the national equivalent).
5. **What documentation can the supplier provide up front?** CoC, export license, and any existing type-approval certificates — request actual documents, not promises.

## Common failure modes

- **Conflating tax clearance with road legality.** Paying duty gets the vehicle *into* the country; approval makes it legal to *drive*. They are separate processes in every market on this page.
- **Assuming brand-level approval covers your vehicle.** Type approvals are variant- and VIN-specific. "The brand sells in Europe" does not mean your specific import is approved.
- **Discovering adaptation requirements after shipment.** Lighting, display, and anchor-point changes are cheapest when scoped before the vehicle leaves China.
- **Using one market's approval as evidence in another.** An EU approval does not automatically satisfy the UK, the Gulf, or Australia. Each regime stands alone.
