# UK Import Guide

The United Kingdom left the EU customs union, which changes the picture for Chinese EV imports in one important way: the EU's countervailing duty does **not** apply in the UK. That makes the UK a meaningfully cheaper destination than the EU for the same vehicle — at least on the tax side.

## The UK cost stack

- **Import duty:** 10% of CIF value (the UK maintains a 10% MFN rate on Chinese EVs).
- **Countervailing duty:** none at the time of writing.
- **VAT:** 20%.

Because there is no countervailing duty, the compounding is simpler: standard duty on CIF, then VAT on the duty-inclusive total.

## A worked example: BYD Seal

The same BYD Seal at $24,690 base price lands quite differently in the UK than in Germany:

| Line item | Amount (USD) |
|---|---|
| Base price | $24,690 |
| Standard duty (10%) | $2,469 |
| Countervailing duty | $0 |
| VAT (20%) | $5,432 |
| RoRo freight | $2,000 |
| Customs clearance | $350 |
| Certification (IVA) | $3,000 |
| Registration | $400 |
| Inland transport | $500 |
| **Total landed** | **$38,841** |

That's a **+57.3%** premium — roughly 22 percentage points cheaper than the equivalent German figure, driven almost entirely by the absence of the countervailing duty.

## UK-specific approval: IVA

The UK has its own vehicle approval regime, the **Individual Vehicle Approval (IVA)** scheme, administered by the DVSA. Categories relevant to EV imports:

- **Basic IVA** — for passenger cars, the most common route for single imports.
- **Mutual recognition** — a vehicle already EU type-approved may qualify for a simplified route, though this depends on the specific approval status.

Like the EU, a China-market EV will likely need adaptations (lighting, speedometer in mph, right-hand-drive considerations) before passing IVA. Budget $1,500–$4,000 and 2–5 weeks.

**Before you ship — three verification steps:**

1. **Check for an existing approval.** Ask the supplier for a UK or EU type-approval certificate by VIN. Mutual recognition is route-specific — do not assume a vehicle "sold in the EU" carries an approval DVSA will accept.
2. **Confirm the IVA category and adaptation list with the inspection centre** before the vehicle leaves China. Lighting and speedometer parts are cheap in the source market and expensive to source after arrival.
3. **Treat the inspection as a test, not a formality.** A failed IVA test adds re-test fees and time; the same adaptations done pre-shipment are the single best cost lever on this line item.

## Practical notes

- **VAT recoverability:** as in the EU, VAT is recoverable for VAT-registered businesses.
- **Currency:** the UK market prices in GBP, so factor in USD/GBP movement over the multi-month import window.
- **No CV duty today, but monitor policy:** trade policy can change; verify current rates at the time of import.
